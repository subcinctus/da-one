Dodge College of Film and Media Arts, MFA in Film and Television Producing

Priority deadline: 15 November 2026 (fellowship preference)

1. Statement of Intent

Express your reasons for wanting to join the program. What are your personal and career goals, and how will this particular degree from Chapman University help you reach those goals?

My family has no connection to film, and while I studied economics, business studies and accountancy at Suncity School in Gurugram, nobody at home knew a career would come out of my camera. I began with what I call the contrast frame, phone edits of family photographs pushed until they stood out, and trips with a camera taught me the balanced frame. It was not until a commercial shoot with a model at the Raghu Rai Center for Photography, where I hid in a corner looking for better frames than the studio set-up allowed, that I began to want an impact frame, one whose meaning matters more than its effect.

The frame first moved with The Classroom, a five-part web series friends and I made at school and put on YouTube. I co-directed, shot and edited it, and because nobody else was going to, I organised it, finding places to shoot, fitting shoot days around exams and keeping classmates involved.

At Whistling Woods International I earned my B.Sc. in Filmmaking, Cinematography and Direction, awarded by TISS, in 2025. I was assistant cinematographer on five larger-scale short films and lead cinematographer on independent films, and so many student sets wanted me as their behind-the-scenes photographer, for stills and video, that I could not cover them all and committed to shoots in advance. I was a camera assistant on Khauf at Matchbox Studios, under Pankaj Kumar, and since October 2025 I have held the same role at Bayview Projects under producer Boney Kapoor. On a Finolex commercial for Early Man Productions I was on the direction team, preparing the PPM deck, production sheets and cast references, and running costume trials, and at Growth Jockey I planned a shoot for Sleepyhead Mattresses: its equipment, script, offer and audience.

Behind the camera or photographing the crew, I kept meeting problems that had started before the shoot, in a schedule or a budget, which nobody holding a camera could fix. I want to work on those myself. I have yet to work in a production office, develop a project with a writer, raise money for it or plan its distribution.

Chapman's MFA teaches that work in sequence: story development, financing, entertainment law, and marketing and distribution. Its thesis asks producers to make advanced films with Dodge's film production students while developing an industry-ready project, the double practice my studio will need to make one project as it develops the next. Marion Knott Studios and the required internship would bring that work close to industry conditions.

My personal goal is to be a producer people trust with their money and their work. As media manager for Mitti to Matka, a COVID initiative for Gurugram's potters, I learned that this means care with money and honesty about what a small team can deliver. My career goal is a creative studio in India of 8 to 30 people, with a slate of its own, that gives paid roles and training to talented people with no industry contacts. I plan to start it once I have spent a few years in development and production, and if everyone who joins it learns, as I hope to at Chapman, to decide what reaches the frame before anyone rolls, the studio will be doing what I am building it for.

3. Project description essay

Describe a large project or event that you planned where you were required to work within a team. Explain some of the challenges you faced, how you handled them, and how the project ultimately turned out.

Before The Classroom, everything I made fitted inside a still photograph, and what went into the frame was mine to decide. The Classroom was the first project where the frame began to move, and where filling it depended on other people's time as much as on mine. It was a five-part web series that a group of friends and I made while we were at Suncity School in Gurugram and released on YouTube, and it is still the largest project I have planned. We produced it ourselves. Besides co-directing, shooting and editing the series, I took on the planning, because nobody else in the group was going to.

Every episode needed somewhere to shoot, and finding those places fell to me. We were all in school, so exams came first, and I set the shoot days in the gaps between them. A shoot could go ahead only when a place, the cast and a free day lined up, and lining them up, episode after episode, was my job.

The team was our classmates, including the actors and a scriptwriter. They were giving their own time to the series, and five episodes is a long commitment for students with exams of their own. Alongside the scheduling, I kept them involved from one episode to the next, because a series that loses its team halfway through is never finished, however well its first episodes turn out.

Fitting shoots between exams meant that every shoot day mattered, and it was on one of those days that the plan gave way. An actor pulled out at the last minute. We did not want to lose the day, so instead of stopping we made up a new character there and then, a funny sports teacher, and wrote him in where the missing role had been. I played him myself.

That left two more problems. The dialogue had been written for the character who was no longer there, and our scriptwriter was not with us, so the two of us, one on set and one at home, rewrote the lines for a sports teacher. The new character also needed a costume, and I found a standard tracksuit that cost us nothing. With the sports teacher written in, the episode's story still held together.

Looking back, that day showed me the half of filmmaking that never appears on screen. The places, the people and the days had all been arranged before we arrived, and when one of those arrangements failed, the answer had to come from the same kind of work: a rewrite, a new role and a costume. Anyone watching the episode sees the sports teacher, not the work that put him there, and that work decided what the camera could see.

We completed all five episodes and released them on YouTube. It was the first project I organised from start to finish.

Since then I have studied cinematography at Whistling Woods International, worked as a camera assistant, and planned a commercial shoot, the Sleepyhead Mattresses campaign for Growth Jockey in April 2024. What I have not yet done is build the budget and the schedule for a professional production. At Chapman I want to learn that, and how a project is developed and financed before anyone plans its shoot, so that the studio I plan to build in India, of 8 to 30 people, can settle its problems on paper long before they reach a shoot day.

Dodge College and Argyros College, joint MBA/MFA in Film and Television Producing

Priority deadline: 15 November 2026

1. Statement of Intent (MBA/MFA)

Express your reasons for wanting to join the joint MBA/MFA. Cover personal and career goals and how this particular dual degree from Chapman will help you achieve them.

In the commerce stream at Suncity School, Gurugram, my subjects were economics, business studies and accountancy, while outside class I taught myself photography, and for years the two seemed unrelated. My eye developed in stages: the contrast frame first, trip photographs cut out and recomposed on my phone; then the balanced frame of landscapes; and at last what I call the impact frame, valued for what it says about the people in it.

The frame kept moving when I approached a musician at school about a shoot. We shot on a secluded hilly road behind the city, liked the results enough to return the next day for a sequence of pictures in which he held still while the background moved, and by the third shoot it was all video. Each shoot was arranged around what the one before had given us, and the arranging mattered as much as the shooting.

At Whistling Woods International I trained as a cinematographer and in 2025 completed the B.Sc. in Filmmaking, Cinematography and Direction, conferred by the Tata Institute of Social Sciences. I assisted the cinematographer on five larger-scale short films, shot independent films as lead cinematographer, and became the go-to behind-the-scenes photographer for student sets, shooting stills and video, with more sets asking than I could cover. I have also been a camera assistant, first on Khauf for Matchbox Studios and, since October 2025, at Bayview Projects, producer Boney Kapoor's company.

Photographing crews and then working beside them, I saw how much of each shoot day had been decided before we arrived, in the schedule, budget, locations and cast, and how a weak decision landed on the set for the camera team to absorb. I have done some of it myself, though never as a producer: preparing the production sheets, cast references and PPM deck and running costume trials on the direction team of Early Man Productions' Finolex commercial, and planning a Sleepyhead Mattresses shoot for Growth Jockey, from its equipment and script to the audience its offer was meant for.

I plan to build a studio in India of between 8 and 30 people that develops its own films and series and pays its people fairly. It will need financing, contracts, a sound structure, distribution partners, cash to last between projects, and producers who treat writers and directors as creative partners. I have not run a company or worked in a production department, and I would rather learn the business and the creative work side by side.

That is why I am applying to Chapman's joint MBA/MFA. At Argyros I would study strategy and financial management, so that I can run the company as well as choose its projects; at Dodge I would take the producing courses, from entertainment law to financing, and produce with student directors and writers. The joint thesis carries one project through rights, pitching, budgeting, packaging, marketing and distribution.

My personal goal is to read a budget as closely as a script. My career goal, after some years in development and production, is the studio. If its films hold the impact frame and its accounts are kept as carefully as its pictures, the commerce classroom and the camera will at last be doing the same job.

2. Argyros Statement of Intent

Discuss how Chapman University and the Argyros College will help you achieve your career and personal goals.

For years my training has been in what goes into a frame. The studio I plan to start in India will also need someone to decide what the company can afford to put there, and I want that to be me. My creative training is in place: a filmmaking degree from Whistling Woods International, awarded by TISS, and my current work as a camera assistant at Bayview Projects. My business training stopped at Suncity School, where I took economics, business studies and accountancy, and I have never run a company.

The nearest I have come is Mitti to Matka, an initiative that supported Gurugram's potters during COVID. Alongside its graphics, stills, video and website, I handled the administration, and because the pots were the families' income, the work made me careful with money.

Argyros would give me formal training in strategy, financial management and leadership, so that I can structure a slate, manage cash across long projects and build a team. Studying it alongside the Dodge MFA means the two kinds of training happen at the same time and meet in the joint thesis, where a project has to hold together in the budget as well as on the page. Personally, I want to read my studio's accounts and make its business decisions myself, so that the people who join it, between 8 and 30 of them, can trust that the company behind their work is run with the care they bring to the frame.
