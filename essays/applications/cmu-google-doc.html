1. Career goals

What are your career goals after completing graduate school?

After graduating I plan to work in development and production at a company that makes films and series for Indian and international audiences, in a role where I can learn how projects are chosen, financed, packaged and sold. So far my experience has been mostly in the camera department, as a camera assistant on Khauf at Matchbox Studios and now at Bayview Projects under producer Boney Kapoor, and from behind the camera I have seen how much of a shoot is settled before anyone reaches the set. I now want to learn the business that decides which shoots happen at all.

After a few years in that work, I plan to build a creative studio in India of 8 to 30 people that develops its own slate of films and series, including projects that can travel between India and the United States. It would be small enough for every project to get real attention, and run as a sound business, with clear budgets, fair contracts and a slate strategy that survives the years between hits. It would also give paid roles to talented people with no family in the industry, train them on the job and trust them with responsibility. I came to film from a commerce classroom and a family with nobody in the industry. If the studio makes work that earns its audience in India and abroad, and gives even a few people an easier way in than I had, it will have been worth building.

2. Prior experience

How have your prior experiences (professional, academic, and/or personal) prepared you for graduate study and informed your career goals?

I studied commerce before I studied film, and it took me years to connect the two. At Suncity School in Gurugram I took economics, business studies and accountancy, but outside class my eye had already moved from what I now call contrast frames, family photographs I edited on a phone to stand out, to balanced frames, landscapes built on composition. It was not until I hid in a corner of a commercial shoot at the Raghu Rai Center for Photography, looking for better frames than the studio set-up allowed, that I understood I wanted impact frames, pictures about people.

The frame began to move with The Classroom, a five-part web series friends and I produced ourselves for YouTube. I co-directed, shot and edited it, and also did what I would now call producing: finding places to shoot, scheduling shoots around our exams and keeping classmates involved.

During COVID I was media manager for Mitti to Matka, which bought and auctioned pots from Gurugram's potters at fair prices after their sales collapsed, and delivered food and sanitary supplies to their families. Besides the media work, I handled the administration, and learned to treat shared money as a trust.

At Whistling Woods International in Mumbai I completed a B.Sc. in Filmmaking, Cinematography and Direction, awarded by the Tata Institute of Social Sciences, in 2025. I was assistant cinematographer on five short films, lead or assistant on independent films, and camera assistant on Khauf at Matchbox Studios under Pankaj Kumar. Student sets kept asking me to shoot their behind-the-scenes stills and video, more than I could cover, so I committed to shoots in advance, and even on days without a set of my own I wanted to be on one, because my heart was in the work of the whole set. On set after set I saw problems that had started earlier, in the schedule or the budget, which the camera team could only work around. Some of my work came before the shoot. On a Finolex commercial for Early Man Productions I was on the direction team, preparing the production sheets, cast references and pre-production meeting deck and running costume trials, and in April 2024 I planned a Sleepyhead Mattresses shoot for Growth Jockey: the equipment, the script, and the offer and audience it was aimed at.

Since October 2025 I have been a camera assistant at Bayview Projects under producer Boney Kapoor. I have not yet worked in a production office, the side I want to learn: how content is developed, financed, budgeted, scheduled, distributed and marketed, and how a production company lasts. I bring a commerce background, a camera department's view of how plans hold up on set, and care with other people's money. I began by editing pictures until they stood out; I want to leave Heinz able to decide early which stories deserve a frame, and then build a studio in India of 8 to 30 people that makes those decisions well.

3. Why Heinz College

How will attending this degree program at Heinz College help you achieve your career goals?

While film school taught me how a film is made, it did not teach me how to use data and financial analysis to decide which film to make, and the studio I plan to build will need financing, contracts and a slate strategy as much as it needs good projects. MEIM would teach me that side of the work in the order I need it. In Pittsburgh I would build the foundations I lack, in production finance, script and story analysis, and negotiation. In Los Angeles I would apply them in production management, film economics, business development and intellectual property, while interning in the industry alongside classes, and I would meet the people and companies who could later partner with a studio in India. The capstone, a team project for a real industry client, would let me work on that client's business problem before I run a company of my own.

At Heinz I would learn to put data and financial analysis behind choosing a slate, pricing a project and understanding an audience. In return I would bring set experience in India and a cinematographer's sense of what things cost on screen. If I leave Heinz able to read a budget as closely as I read a frame, the studio I build after my years in development will stand on sound numbers.
