# Karan's story, chapter by chapter (from photo_story_verbatim.md, facts_addendum.md, facts_check.md; binding)

**1. Phones and editing (from 2016).** Like many boys in 2016 he was obsessed with phones and their cameras, Huawei's above all.
- Name a model only as "Huawei P20 Pro", which dates to 2018, so never tie it to 2016. Leave out the US sanctions.
- His editing went from filters and adjustments to adding graphic elements. The first thing he taught himself was object selection and cutouts: blending in new backgrounds and highlighting the focus of an image "in an exaggerated stylish way".
- He edited every picture from his family's trips and had them all printed.
- These were his "contrast frames", built to stand out.

**2. Picking up a camera.** Only when he got bored of zooming into images on a phone did he pick up a camera.
- It reminded him of his failed stint with Filmora, a video editor, so the camera felt like a new achievement.
- He taught himself the camera until there was nothing on it he hadn't tried. Never write "mastery".

**3. Dehradun (2017/2018).** On trips to Dehradun, where his sibling and two cousins were at school at Hopetown, he shot with a Canon 600D and its kit lens.
- Never name or gender the sibling.
- He later traded it in for a nifty fifty (50mm f/1.8) with a 7D Mark II. In his words, the combination "opened possibilities beyond what a better camera could offer". Its autofocus was built for sport and wildlife.
- With the 7D he used manual focus creatively for the first time and played with bokeh. He learned how stabilisers work, and asked himself why his camera had no in-body stabilisation when Sony offered it for less.

**4. Monaco (with family; DO NOT date it or name a race).**
- He taught himself macro photography, manual HDR blending, and long exposures of a changing sky. These were the "symmetrical and balanced frames" of landscapes.
- He caught an F1 car flat out on the straight, from above, dead centre in the frame. NO speed figure.
- With an 18-135mm at full zoom he caught seagulls soaring about fifty metres away.

**5. The Raghu Rai Center for Photography (2019 to 2020, about eight months).**
- It is the school founded by the photographer Raghu Rai. Raghu Rai died on 26 April 2026: at most one respectful clause, in at most one essay per application. Never "Academy", never "prestigious".
- He studied under Nitin Rai, Raghu Rai's son, who is alive. Use Karan's own account.
- Nitin saw that Karan loved media creation and a free-moving camera far more than the fixed-camera fashion shoots he had spent months teaching. Say "months", not "a year".
- On one assignment, when Nitin had called in a model for a commercial shoot, Karan hid in a corner trying to get better pictures than the Vogue-style studio set-up. He got some beautiful frames, "and so did he". Keep that generosity.
- In his last month there he invited a friend to model for him. She was a complete amateur, and he got three outfits done in two hours.
- After it he was, in his own words, "probably way too confident". Use this as a self-aware, warm line.

**6. Family.** His family never knew this would become his career. Work wasn't something his parents talked to the children about until his senior years at school. Today the family's houses are full of his polished prints. He sometimes misses the amateur auto-exposure moments when everyone was made to smile, but prefers candids now, "because now the images bring smiles".

**7. The Classroom (this came BEFORE the musician: in Karan's words "the youtube series being my first exposure to video").** His first real video work, a five-part web series made with friends while at Suncity School. He co-directed, shot, edited and organised it. Never give years. After it he found confidence. The sports-teacher story belongs in teamwork or problem prompts only, with exact facts.

**8. The musician (after The Classroom and after the Raghu Rai course, when he was "probably way too confident"; 11th grade; never name him).**
- A musician at school, then making things for fun like Karan and now steadily releasing and producing, blew Karan's mind with his music.
- They shot "many, many pictures" on a secluded hilly road outside the city. The musician loved them.
- The very next day they shot again, at the musician's request, for a sequence made of stills in which he stays steady and the background moves. Today that would be done with Gaussian splats.
- He loved it so much that he wouldn't risk a new location, so they went back once more. This time it was all video: an intro of him driving in, opening the back of the car, and a graphic title at the top.
- This is where his photography and his video met in work for someone else: stills turned into a moving sequence, then into a shoot that was all video. Do NOT call it his first video; The Classroom was.

**9. SHLAY.**
- A professional shoot for a senior's new boutique clothing brand, SHLAY. The model graduated from his school before he joined middle school.
- By now he had moved to a Nikon Z6 with a heavy 24-70mm. He had been convinced the camera was doing the work, until he handed it to someone else and saw that knowledge made the difference.
- He now felt the urge for a meaningful frame: not the contrast frame of his editing days, not the symmetrical, balanced frame of landscapes, but an "impact frame".
- In Karan's account the shoot was the day before he left for Europe. Keep that, but never date it.

**10. France.** He hit a point where he could not stop using the camera, "not because of the architecture, not because of the camera".
- He shot on autopilot: motion long exposures, frozen water, birds, strangers, and his own family as if on black-and-white film.
- He had built colour profiles before travelling and never felt the need to edit a frame.
- The trip settled for him the difference between a career and a passion.

**11. Whistling Woods.** The go-to behind-the-scenes photographer, stills and video, for student sets.
- Asked by more sets than he could cover, so he committed in advance.
- Even on days with no set of his own, he wanted to be on one, for beautiful sets, people at work and candid moments.
- Shooting the people making films, not the film, is the bridge to producing.
- He was lead cinematographer on independent films and assistant cinematographer on five larger-scale short films.

**12. Now.** A camera assistant who sees that problems reaching the camera started earlier. He wants to be where those decisions are made: producing, and a studio in India of 8 to 30 people.
