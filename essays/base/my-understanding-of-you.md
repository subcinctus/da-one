#### (b) Mottos: how they were researched and used

- **Method:** "Researched 2026-10-11 using WebSearch only. WebFetch failed with DNS errors on every domain tried (wikipedia.org, cmu.edu), so all quotes below come from search-result excerpts of the pages cited. "Confirmed" means an official institutional page carries the wording. "Unconfirmed" means it appears only on secondary sources. "Unknown" means nothing was found." (mottos.md)
- **General rule:** "a motto works when it names something the applicant has actually done, and only once. Don't open with it and don't translate it for the reader as a lesson. Leave out religious mottos unless the applicant genuinely shares that faith context." (mottos.md)
- **Used or avoided:** "used" below means the phrase appears in the answers doc on 12 Oct 2026. That was checked by searching the file for each motto and its key words; the line numbers are given.
- **Reviewer criticism.** A review of the published docs flagged two of the motto lines: "MACHINE-MADE, p.2 (SOP): 'Fiat Lux, the University of California's motto, is also a cinematographer's instruction; at UCLA I want to learn to give it earlier, at the script.' #### Real-world facts it rests on

- **Glacier grafting is real.** Villagers in Baltistan and Gilgit-Baltistan pair "male" and "female" ice.
  - NPR reports that villagers in Chunda "carried the glacier chunks up a mountain in silence", and that a cleric "put the male glacier chunks atop this bed, with the female pieces added after" (web: https://www.nhpr.org/2023-09-03/a-glacier-baby-is-born-mating-glaciers-to-replace-water-lost-to-climate-change).
  - "Grey-colored glaciers are considered male glaciers while white-colored glaciers are identified as female glaciers." The Balti terms are "Mo-gang" (female) and "Po-gang" (male) (web: https://thefridaytimes.com/08-Jan-2024/glacier-grafting-as-a-solution-for-water-scarcity).
  - Grafting needs "a piece each of a 'male' and 'female' glacier weighing approximately 35 kilogrammes". The practice is called "Gang Khswa", and "after ten or 12 years, these efforts are supposed to birth a glacier" (web: https://dialogue.earth/en/?p=20033794; web: https://thekarakoram.com.pk/?p=733).
  - These facts match the piece: "White female ice and grey male ice", "carried uphill in silence", "thirty-five kilos of bride", and "in ten winters or so a glacier grows" (work_all/01.md Q6).
  - **Not confirmed:**
    - The rule that the ice may "never [be] set down or stopped". The search summaries mention silence, but not this rule.
    - That the rite is practised in Turtuk itself. The sources describe it in Pakistani Baltistan and Gilgit-Baltistan.
    - Charcoal and sawdust. NPR mentions "insulating chaff and coal", which is close but not the same.
- **Turtuk came under India in December 1971.**
  - "On 16 December 1971, Turtuk became a part of India after the Indian Army captured it." Another account says villagers "went to sleep in Pakistan but woke up in Hindustan" on the night of 13 December (web: https://villagesquare.in/turtuk-villagers-piece-together-their-broken-pasts/; web: https://m.thewire.in/article/123835/turtuk-story-of-a-promise-land/amp).
  - "Almost every household here has relatives on the other side of the LoC." In 2014, four people reached Skardu after 43 years to see their families (web: https://pamirtimes.net/2014/03/29/skardu-families-separated-1971-war-reunite-43-years/).
  - Divided families keep in touch through WhatsApp groups (web: https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-patiala/20170831/281848643741487). This supports the patched-through call in the piece, though the call itself is fiction.
- **Frano is a real village across the Line.**
  - "Frano is a Pakistani border village in Ghanche District, Gilgit-Baltistan... on the north bank of the Shyok River, with Turtuk on the south side... considered the last village of Pakistan, with the LoC 2.5 km away" (web: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frano,_Gilgit-Baltistan).
  - Turtuk "sits on the border overlooking the Shyok River, which flows into Pakistan-administered Baltistan" (web: https://www.outlooktraveller.com/destinations/india/from-the-latest-issue-inside-turtuk-indias-final-village-before-the-loc). The last line of the piece rests on this.
- **Thang is real.**
  - It is described as "the northernmost and last village of India, situated along the Line of Control", "about 4km from Turtuk", but "tourists are not allowed to visit the village" (web: https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/lg-kavinder-visits-thang-indias-northernmost-border-village-in-turtuk-nubra/; web: https://thelandofwanderlust.com/thang-border-ladakh-travel-guide-the/).
  - **Caution:** Ali sells binocular looks "at the Thang viewpoint", and his taxi "drives the road to Thang and back" (work_all/01.md Q6). One source says tourists may go only as far as Tyakshi. Treat the viewpoint business as fiction that may not match current access rules.
- **The two armies do exchange sweets on festivals.**
  - Indian and Pakistani troops have exchanged sweets on Eid and Diwali along the LoC. Examples include Eid in May 2021 at Tithwal and Kaman-Aman Setu, and Diwali in November 2021 at Chakan Da Bagh, Poonch (web: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/india-pak-armies-exchange-sweets-at-loc-on-eid-ul-fitr-252656; web: https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/j-k/indian-pak-soldiers-exchange-sweets-562095/amp).
  - **Not confirmed:** any such exchange at a brigade flag meeting in Ladakh. The examples found are all in Jammu and Kashmir.

#### Real-world facts it rests on

- **SITE ran for exactly one year in about 2,400 villages.**
  - "SITE ran from 1 August 1975 to 31 July 1976, reaching more than 2,400 villages in 20 districts across six Indian states and territories." It was "designed jointly by NASA and the Indian Space Research Organization (ISRO)" (web: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_Instructional_Television_Experiment, via search summary).
  - NASA's pre-launch note: ATS-6 "will receive C-band video signals from Ahmedabad and Delhi, India, and will retransmitt the video... at 860 MHz to 6 clusters of 400 direct-receive stations for a total of 2400 direct-receive stations" (web: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760034939).
  - Programmes: "Morning programs of 1.5 hours per day are designed for classroom use, and evening programs of 2.5 hours duration are designed for village adult education" (web: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760034939). Gopal's skipped "morning school broadcast" and the "evening one" match this.
  - Orissa was one of the SITE states, and one source names a production centre at Cuttack (web: https://www.shcollege.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/NAAC_Documents_IV_Cycle/Criterion-II/2.3.2/HistoryofTV.pdf).

  - **Not confirmed:** "ten-foot chicken-wire dish". Two searches found nothing on chicken-wire or mesh dishes. The advisor states it as fact, but no source was found in this pass. A Sambalpur SITE cluster is also **not confirmed**.
- **ATS-6 was parked over Lake Victoria.** In the pilot, the satellite is "staying above a lake in Africa".
  - "From the end of July 1975 until August 1976... the satellite was brought into a position above Victoria Lake at 35° East" (web: https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03003503).
  - A NASA report says it was moved "Starting 1 August 1976... from 35°E" (web: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19800016019/downloads/19800016019.pdf).
- **The dish direction is plausible.** The pilot says "west-south-west, thirty degrees up". This is my own calculation, not a web source. It uses Sambalpur at about 21.5°N, 84°E and a geostationary satellite at 35°E, so the longitude difference is 49°.
  - Elevation comes out at about 30°.
  - Azimuth comes out at about 252°, which is west-south-west.
  - This agrees with the piece and with the advisor's claim.
- **The Emergency and forced sterilisation are real.**
  - The 1975-77 campaign is described as one of the "darkest legacies of the emergency (1975-77) ... led by Sanjay Gandhi". The Shah Commission figures run "1.3 million vasectomies (1975) → 2.6 million (1976) → 8.3 million (1977)". People were "randomly taken from their homes, fields, railway stations and cinema halls, and brought to camps" (web: https://laex.in/articles-editorials/the-forced-sterilisations-of-emergency/; web: https://homegrown.co.in/homegrown-explore/the-dark-history-of-indias-sterilization-and-family-planning-policies).
  - The health minister Karan Singh says Sanjay Gandhi raised the targets (web: https://www.deccanherald.com/india/north-indian-cms-went-overboard-on-sterilisation-targets-because-of-sanjay-gandhi-karan-singh-3922559).

  - Rath Yatra "commemorates Jagannath's annual visit to Gundicha Temple via Mausi Maa Temple (maternal aunt's home)" (web: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratha_Yatra_(Puri); web: https://thegaudiyatreasuresofbengal.com/2022/01/15/mausi-maa-temple-jagannath-puri/).
  - The deities stay for a set period and then go home, which matches the piece's "He is only visiting".
- **Meher as a weaver surname:** the advisor says "Meher is a Sambalpuri weaver surname" (journal wf_e4f1fe36-8c3, line 2). **Not checked on the web.**
